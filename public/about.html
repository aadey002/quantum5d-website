Built by an FQHC Executive

Dr. Adetoro Oriaifo

The problem with most healthcare technology is that it was built by people who have never run an FQHC.

They understand the data. They do not understand what happens when a covered entity gets a split-claim dispute the week before an HRSA site visit. They do not understand what it costs — in staff time, in revenue, in board confidence — when Medicaid redetermination runs through a spreadsheet managed by two eligibility workers who are already at capacity.

The workflows that FQHCs actually need do not come from a product roadmap built in a conference room. They come from years inside the operation — running multi-site pharmacy programs, preparing for OSV audits, presenting to boards who do not yet understand what 340B is, watching eligible patients lose coverage because no system caught the signal early enough.

Quantum5D.ai was built from that experience. Every application on this platform reflects a problem that was real, recurring, and expensive — not a use case that tested well in a demo.

Why AI — and why now

The operational complexity facing FQHCs in 2026 has outpaced what spreadsheets, EHR reports, and manual workflows can manage. Medicaid work requirements. IRA Part D negotiation pressure on 340B margins. PBM reform implementation. HRSA audit protocol changes. Managed care carve-in dynamics that create duplicate discount exposure nobody is tracking.

These are not problems that need more staff. They need systems that reason across data, surface what matters, and keep a human in the decision seat.

That is what this platform is designed to do — and why every application is built with human review at the center, not automated away from it.

Background
